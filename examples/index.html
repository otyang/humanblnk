Examples

Every use case of humanblnk, a wallet and financial-operations domain layer for Go built on top of Blnk, with full code and the real output. Each page shows every way of doing the task, the options, the entries it makes in Blnk, and the errors to expect.

The code calls two handles: wallet := svc.Wallet() for what customers do and ops := svc.Ops() for what the business does. New here? Start with Setup, then Create customer accounts.

Setup and integration

Connect to Blnk, handle money and errors, and wire the library into your app.

Install and connectAdd the module, connect to Blnk Core or Blnk Cloud, check the setup at start-up, and hand each part of your app the one handle it needs.Money and amountsPass amounts as the decimal the user typed. Every amount comes back exact, in both forms Blnk stores, with a display string for people.ErrorsEvery error the library returns, how to check for it with errors.Is, what to tell the customer, and which ones are worth retrying.WebhooksReceive Blnk's events: verify the signature, decode the payload into receipts and alerts, and handle each delivery once.Logging, metrics and tracingWrap the service once with ledger.Instrument and every wallet and ops call is logged, counted or traced, with its name and its error.Testing your appTest the code that calls humanblnk against an in-memory Blnk Core: control the clock, expire holds, run scheduled transfers and assert the webhooks Blnk would send.

Wallet

What customers do: open accounts, add money, pay, cash out, hold, save, buy, dispute.

Create customer accountsOpen accounts for people and businesses: one call for a whole customer, single accounts, savings pots and escrows, all safe to retry.Deposits and top-upsCredit a customer when money arrives from outside: card top-ups, bank transfers and crypto, with fees, safe webhook retries and deposits held for review.Transfers between customersMove money from one account to another: instant, with a fee, held until both sides agree, scheduled for later, or to and from the business's own accounts.Withdrawals and payoutsSend money out to a bank or wallet through a provider: reserved while the provider works, confirmed or cancelled from its webhook, and cancelled by itself if the provider never answers.Card holds and captureReserve money on an account like a card authorisation, then capture all or part of it, release the rest, or let it lapse on a timer.Quotes for confirm screensShow the customer the fee, the total and their balance after, and whether the payment will go through, before anything moves.Currency exchangeConvert money between a customer's accounts in two currencies at your rate, with exact rounding, an optional fee, and both sides posted together or not at all.Split payments and marketplacesPay one amount out to several accounts in a single transaction: a seller, a rider and Oja's commission, with every share computed exactly.RefundsSend money back after a payment: in full in one step, or in parts, with over-refunds refused and every refund linked to the payment it reverses.EscrowTake a buyer's money now and pay the seller only on delivery, in full or in parts, with refunds to the buyer from the same pot.DisputesFreeze the money in question at the recipient while a complaint is looked into, then send it back to the payer or release it, in full or in part.Savings pots and standing ordersLet customers put money aside in named pots with a goal, and move money on a schedule: a monthly savings plan, a weekly allowance, a subscription.Bulk payouts and payrollSend up to 10,000 transfers in one call: payroll that pays everyone or no one, batches held for approval, and big runs processed in the background.Fees and pricingCharge for deposits, withdrawals, transfers and exchanges: a fee on each call, or one pricing function for the whole app, with every fee posted to @FeeIncome.Limits and KYC tiersCap what each customer can send per transaction, per day and per month, by KYC tier and currency, and show them what they have left.Statements and historyShow a customer's activity, build a monthly statement that is checked against Blnk, export it as CSV, and read a balance at any moment.

Financial operations

What the business does: ledgers, its own accounts, controls, settlement and the books.

Provision ledgersCreate the Blnk ledgers your customer accounts live in, safely and once, and check at start-up that the app points at the right one.House accounts and the chart of accountsRead the business's own accounts, such as @FeeIncome and @PaystackClearing, know what each one means in the books, and add or rename your own.Freeze and unfreezeStop money moving on one account, or on a whole ledger, while you investigate, then lift the freeze. Nothing is reversed and no balance changes.Close an accountClose a customer account for good: sweep what is left to another account or out through a provider, with a reason and a second person's approval.Provider settlementRecord what Paystack paid into your bank and what your bank paid Flutterwave, so each provider's clearing account shows exactly what is still outstanding.Unidentified moneyPark money that arrived but can't be matched to a customer in @Suspense, then pay it to its owner or send it back to the provider.Manual adjustmentsCorrect a customer balance by hand, for cashback, goodwill or a posting mistake, with a reason, a requester and a second person who approves it.Trial balance and reportsProve the books add up, see what you owe customers and what the business holds, and close the month with checked statements for any house account.Balance alertsHave Blnk tell you when a balance crosses a line: a customer running low, a provider owing too much, a payout waiting too long.ReconciliationMatch a provider's report against the ledger line by line, with Blnk's matching rules, and find the payments that are in one and not the other.

humanblnk: a wallet and financial-operations domain layer for Go, built on top of Blnk. By Otyang.

Every sample on this page runs against the library's in-memory Blnk Core when the tests run; the output shown is what it printed. In the samples, check(err) stands for your own error handling, and ledger and currency are github.com/otyang/humanblnk/ledger and github.com/otyang/humanblnk/currency.
