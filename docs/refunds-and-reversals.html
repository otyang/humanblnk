Chapter 8 · Moving money

Refunds and reversals

Scenario: Ada pays a shop ₦15,000 for three items. One comes back (a ₦5,000 refund), the support agent clicks "refund" twice, and then the order is cancelled. Separately, a payout to Ada's bank bounces.

Refunds, corrections, chargebacks and bounced payouts all use one call: Transactions.Reverse. It moves money back the way an entry moved it.

The order

A partial refund

Reversal field
Entrythe entry id (Entry.ID from Record)
Amountthe zero Money undoes all of it, or all that's left
Refyour id for this reversal. Required for a partial one, recommended for a full one.
Reasonrequired, for the audit trail

Clicked twice: the same refund

Reverse is idempotent on Ref. The second click returns the refund already made, and no money moves:

Without a Ref, a retried full reversal can only answer ErrAlreadyReversed: it can't tell its own refund from someone else's. Always pass one.

Never more than was paid

Reversals of one entry, full and partial, can never add up to more than it moved, even when two run at the same time (they take turns).

Cancel the rest

A full reversal after partial ones undoes only what's left. Once nothing is left, Reverse is ErrAlreadyReversed.

A bounced payout

Good to know

  • A refund takes money from where it went. If the shop had already spent the ₦15,000, the refund would fail with ErrInsufficientFunds: a customer is never pushed below zero. System accounts (@World, @FeeIncome, clearing accounts) may go negative.
  • Splits reverse in full only. Reversing a transfer with a fee undoes both postings in one entry. Which postings of a split to undo in part is a business decision: reverse it in full, then record what should stand.
  • Only applied entries can be reversed. For money still in a hold, use Holds.Cancel.
  • Each reversal is its own entry, referenced reversal:<entry>:<n> and tagged reversal_of, reversal_ref and reversal_reason in its metadata.
