Chapter 3 · Foundations

Money

Scenario: a price of ₦1,500.50, a 1.5% fee, and the total that goes into the ledger and out as JSON.

money.Money is an exact amount in one currency. It is never a float: amounts are parsed from the decimal someone wrote and kept exact. Every amount you give the ledger is a money.Money. This chapter needs no Blnk.

Currencies

Parsing

Parsing is strict, so a bad amount is caught at the edge of your system.

InputResult
"1500.50", "-3", "1500"fine
"1500.505"ErrTooManyDecimals: NGN has 2
"1e3", "1,500", ".5", "+3"ErrInvalidAmount

money.MustParse panics instead of returning an error. Use it only for constants in your own code, never for what a client sent.

Arithmetic

  • Add, Sub and Cmp work only within one currency; anything else is money.ErrCurrencyMismatch.
  • Compare with Equal or Cmp, never ==. 1.5 and 1.50 USD are Equal.
  • Also: IsPositive, IsNegative, IsZeroAmount, Sign, Neg, Abs, Amount (the decimal), Code, Currency, Valid.

The smallest unit

Ledgers store integers: kobo, cents, wei. Minor and FromMinor convert exactly, even for 18-decimal crypto.

Rounding: only where you ask

Nothing rounds by itself. Where you need it, you choose the direction.

ModeUse for
money.Upa fee HumanBlnk charges: never undercharge by a fraction of a kobo
money.Downan amount paid out or credited: never pay out more than exists
money.HalfEvenreports

JSON

The amount is a string, so nothing in between turns it into a float. Decoding is strict: exactly amount and currency, with the code in capitals.
