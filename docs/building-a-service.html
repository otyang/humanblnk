Chapter 7 · Moving money

Building a service: airtime

Scenario: Ada buys ₦1,000 of airtime (prepaid mobile phone credit) from her wallet. The airtime provider (VTpass, say) can deliver, decline, or not answer in time.

This chapter shows the shape every business feature takes: its own package that imports humanblnk, never the other way round. humanblnk knows nothing about airtime; the airtime package decides what to take and when.

The design: a hold around the provider call

The provider is outside your books and can fail or time out, so the money is held first, not taken outright:

  1. Hold the amount. If Ada can't afford it, stop before calling the provider.
  2. Call the provider.
  3. Delivered: capture. Declined: cancel. No clear answer: leave it held, and settle it when the provider's webhook or a status check answers.

The money goes to the provider's clearing account, humanblnk.ClearingAccount("vtpass"): what you owe the provider, or how much of a prepaid float is used.

Step 1: depend on interfaces

The package names the small part of humanblnk it uses, so tests can pass a fake. hb.Holds satisfies Holds.

Step 2: buy

Buy places the hold, calls the provider, then settles. It's safe to call again with the same Purchase: Place returns the same hold, and a full capture can't move money twice.

Step 3: settle

Settle captures or cancels. The provider's webhook handler calls it for a purchase that was pending.

What a real service adds

  • Its own table (purchase id, wallet, phone, amount, hold id, status), so the webhook and a retry job can find pending purchases and call Settle.
  • A provider that is safe to retry: send your purchase id as the provider's request id, so a retry never sends airtime twice.
  • No hold expiry: if Blnk expired the hold while the provider had in fact delivered, the airtime would be free.
  • A fee or commission: make To a split with humanblnk.Many(...). Splits capture only in full, which is all Settle does.
